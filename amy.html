Amy
— Cultural Gaps

A digital storytelling project from the "Cultural Gaps" series — telling Amy Winehouse's life, her struggles, her cultural impact, and her connection to the 27 Club.

RoleCreative Designer
SeriesCultural Gaps
FormatStorycard Experience
ToolsDesign + AI Ideation

Project Overview

Telling a life, not just a legacy.

Amy Winehouse's story carries an unusual amount of cultural weight — a singular voice, a public struggle, and a place in the "27 Club" that has been retold many times over. The brief for Cultural Gaps was to give that story a visual form that felt honest rather than sensational.

The final product is a Storycard digital experience: designed banners, a scrolling narrative page, layered visual backgrounds, considered typography, and editorial content working together as one continuous read.

My Role

Creative Designer

  • Visual concept development
  • Art direction across the full experience
  • Building the visual language for the "Cultural Gaps" series
  • Banner design
  • Digital storytelling design
  • AI-assisted ideation

Creative Process

From research to a defensible direction.

The process moved in one direction, with room to circle back once real research entered the picture.

Idea→ Research→ Creative Direction→ AI Exploration→ Design Refinement→ Final Experience

AI entered the process as a creative partner rather than a shortcut — used specifically to widen the range of directions considered before any of them were designed by hand.

  • GPT for early idea exploration and framing questions about tone
  • Gemini for comparing creative directions side by side
  • Google creative tools for palette exploration

AI supports the creative process. It does not replace human creative direction.

Visual Direction

A palette built from contrast, not decoration.

The visual language leans on deep tonal backgrounds against soft, editorial type — giving the heavier subject matter room to breathe rather than dramatizing it. Backgrounds were treated as atmosphere, letting the story's own content carry the emotional weight.

Final Experience

A scroll that reads like a story, not a slideshow.

The finished Storycard experience unfolds as a single continuous scroll — designed banners marking chapters of Amy's life, paired with editorial copy and a visual rhythm that slows down at the heaviest moments and lifts at the cultural ones.

Reflection

What this project proved.

Cultural Gaps confirmed something I've carried into every project since: AI is most useful early, when a direction is still soft, and least useful at the point where craft and judgment need to take over. The strongest part of this project isn't the tooling — it's the editorial discipline of knowing when to stop exploring and start designing.
